WEEK 1 · LESSON 1

What does a TPM actually do?

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

A TPM creates structure around a complex technical goal and drives multiple teams toward a successful outcome.

DEFINE→PLAN→ALIGN→EXECUTE→UNBLOCK→COMMUNICATE

1 · Start with discovery

Before building a schedule, understand the program.

WHY → WHAT → WHO → TECH → DEPENDENCIES → METRICS → TIME → RISKS

Ask why the program exists, what must be delivered, who owns or depends on it, how the technology works, how success is measured, and what could prevent delivery.

2 · Establish O-S-S-M

Objective
Why are we doing this?
Scope
What exactly are we delivering?
Stakeholders
Who owns, influences or depends on this?
Metrics
How will we know we succeeded?

3 · Scope ≠ schedule

Scope is WHAT we committed to deliver. Schedule is WHEN we committed to deliver it.

Example: “100% of telemetry migrated” is scope/completion criteria. “Within six months” is schedule.

If a team proposes 70% now and 30% later, the TPM surfaces that as a change to the original commitment.

4 · Know the owners

Executive sponsor
Senior leader supporting major organizational escalations.
Service owner
Team accountable for the platform or system.
Producer / consumer owners
Teams sending or receiving data.
Decision owner
Person authorized to approve a tradeoff.

A TPM does not make every decision. A TPM gets the right decision to the right owner with the right information.

5 · Manage dependencies

A dependency is work that must happen before another outcome can happen.

Azure scaling fix↓Load test↓Security validation↓100% migration

Ask: Who owns it? When is it needed? What happens if it slips?

6 · Move risk discovery left

Find important problems as early as possible. In the exercise, production-scale load testing exposed a scaling limit before full migration.

EARLY VALIDATION → EARLIER MITIGATION → LOWER PROGRAM RISK

7 · Turn technical problems into decisions

PROBLEM→EVIDENCE→IMPACT→OPTIONS→RECOMMENDATION→DECISION
Example

Problem: Peak volume exceeds demonstrated platform performance.

Evidence: 9M events/sec succeeds; at 12M, 1.5% arrives late.

Impact: Security will not approve full migration.

Options: Delay, phase 70/30, or find another safe mitigation.

Recommendation: Phase the migration when Security and dependent teams validate the approach.

Decision: Leadership approves any change to the original program commitment.

8 · Communicate at the right altitude

Engineers may need detailed test results. Leadership usually needs the decision-ready version:

What's wrong?What's the impact?What are our options?What do you recommend?Who needs to decide?
LESSON 1 CHEAT CODE

What happened? → Why does it matter? → What can we do? → What do I recommend? → Who decides?

WEEK 1 · LESSON 2

Turning a goal into an executable program.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

A TPM turns an outcome into coordinated execution by defining workstreams, milestones, owners, dependencies, risks, mitigations and decision points.

OUTCOME→WORKSTREAMS→MILESTONES→OWNERS→DEPENDENCIES→EXECUTION

1 · Build workstreams around outcomes

Break a large program into logical areas with clear ownership and deliverables.

WORKSTREAM → OWNER → DELIVERABLE

For the migration exercise: Platform Scaling, Data Pipeline Preparation, Security Validation, Operational Readiness and Phase 1 Migration.

2 · Milestones are not tasks

A task is work someone performs. A milestone is a meaningful program state.

Task: run load tests. Milestone: production capacity validated.

Milestones tell leadership what important state the program has reached.

3 · Trace dependencies downstream

When one team slips, do not stop at the local delay. Trace what it blocks.

Data pipeline ready↓Security validation↓Phase 1 migration

A dependency delay can propagate through the program even when other teams remain on schedule.

4 · Find the critical path

The critical path is the chain of dependent work that determines the earliest program completion date.

Ask: Which work can actually move our completion date?

Not every task deserves the same level of attention.

5 · Parallelize when dependencies allow

One incompatible schema did not need to block validation of the other nineteen.

19 SCHEMAS → SECURITY VALIDATION IN PARALLEL
SCHEMA #20 → REMEDIATION

Fast-tracking can recover schedule, but the TPM must understand the additional risk it introduces.

6 · Communicate uncertainty precisely

Do not call a milestone Green just because a mitigation exists.

Green
On track; no material threat to the commitment.
Yellow
Risk exists, but a credible mitigation or recovery plan is active.
Red
Commitment is materially off track or blocked.

7 · Make tradeoffs visible

SCOPE↔SCHEDULE↔RESOURCES↔QUALITY / RISK
Resource conflict example

Problem: Schema #20 needed two engineers from another program.

Our impact without help: approximately two-week migration delay.

Other program impact: approximately three-day internal milestone shift while its Week 10 launch remained protected by contingency.

Recommendation: temporarily allocate the engineers because the organizational impact was lower and both external commitments remained protected.

8 · Escalate decisions, not frustration

Escalate when a material program decision cannot be resolved at the current level and requires authority you do not have.

ProblemEvidenceImpactRecommendationDecision needed

Good escalation makes leadership decision-ready.

9 · Give executive-ready status

Compress the program into what leadership needs to know.

STATUS → RISK → MITIGATION → FORECAST / NEXT STEP

Phase 1 remains achievable for Week 7, with Schema #20 as the primary risk. Additional engineering support is addressing compatibility while Security validates the other schemas in parallel. SRE readiness is complete; the Azure scaling fix remains on track for Week 8 before peak-volume validation and Phase 2.

LESSON 2 CHEAT CODE

Outcome → Workstreams → Milestones → Owners → Dependencies → Critical Path → Risks → Mitigation → Tradeoffs → Decisions

WEEK 1 · LESSON 3

Program control with RAID + decision management.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

A TPM keeps execution under control by making uncertainty, active problems, dependencies and time-sensitive decisions visible, owned and actionable.

IDENTIFY→CLASSIFY→OWN→MITIGATE→DECIDE→FOLLOW UP

1 · Know RAID

Risk
Something that may happen and affect the program.
Assumption
Something the plan currently treats as true but has not fully validated.
Issue
A problem that has already happened or is currently true.
Dependency
Work, approval or a decision required for another outcome to proceed.
MAY HAPPEN → RISK · EXPECT / BELIEVE → ASSUMPTION · HAS HAPPENED → ISSUE · NEEDS SOMETHING ELSE → DEPENDENCY

2 · Score risks with evidence

Assess probability and impact separately. A high-impact risk is not automatically high probability.

Ask what evidence supports the probability rating and what commitment is affected if the risk materializes.

3 · Mitigation ≠ contingency

Mitigation reduces probability or impact before a risk occurs. Contingency defines what the team will do if the risk occurs.

Mitigation: test scaling improvements incrementally. Contingency: hold at the validated workload and reforecast Phase 2 if full scaling is not ready.

4 · Convert materialized risks into issues

If a committed Week 8 milestone is officially reforecast to Week 10, the uncertainty about missing Week 8 is gone.

Risk: Azure may miss Week 8↓Trigger: Week 8 no longer achievable↓Issue: current forecast is Week 10

Preserve the history: mark the original risk materialized/closed and track the active issue plus any new downstream risks.

5 · Re-plan from the new facts

A delay does not always mean the whole program must wait. In the exercise, partial scaling supported a validated 10.5M events/sec.

Security approved testing at the intermediate capacity, creating an option to migrate additional telemetry safely while the full storage fix continued.

6 · Decisions can be dependencies

A decision made after its needed-by date can cause the same damage as a late technical deliverable.

DECISION → OWNER → OPTIONS → RECOMMENDATION → NEEDED-BY DATE → OUTCOME → FOLLOW-UP

Track decision latency instead of allowing approvals to sit invisibly in inboxes.

7 · RAID log artifact

Example RAID entry

Risk: Azure scaling remediation may miss the committed Week 8 milestone.

Probability: Medium, pending Azure confidence assessment.

Impact: High — Phase 2 and final migration depend on validated 12M capacity.

Owner: Azure Platform.

Mitigation: Track intermediate milestones, test improvements incrementally, phase work where safe, and identify resources that can accelerate critical-path work.

Trigger: Intermediate milestone missed or confidence in Week 8 materially drops.

Contingency: Maintain the validated workload, evaluate additional safe migration, reforecast Phase 2 and escalate options.

8 · Decision log artifact

Example decision

Decision: Proceed with intermediate migration from 70% → 85%.

Decision owner: Engineering Director.

Options: Remain at 70% until full remediation, or migrate to the validated 85% level.

Recommendation: Proceed to 85%.

Rationale: Earlier legacy cost reduction, additional production learning, increased confidence and a smaller final cutover.

Follow-up: Complete storage remediation → 12M validation → Security approval → 100% migration.

9 · Escalate decision latency with impact

Do not send a generic reminder when a decision is time-critical. Restate the recommendation, readiness, needed-by date and consequence of waiting.

Decision ownerNeeded-by dateRecommendationImpact of delayNext action

Trace the impact before claiming that a delay threatens the final program deadline.

LESSON 3 CHEAT CODE

Risk may happen · Issue has happened · Mitigate before · Contingency after · Decisions have owners and deadlines

WEEK 1 · LESSON 4

Program charter, success metrics + change control.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

A TPM turns an ambiguous goal into an agreed program baseline: why the work matters, what is in and out of scope, how success is measured, who owns what, which constraints matter, and how changes will be evaluated.

WHY→WHAT→SUCCESS→WHO→WHEN→CONSTRAINTS

1 · Define the problem before the solution

The legacy telemetry platform is expensive to operate, difficult to scale as volume grows, and requires significant engineering maintenance.

The problem statement explains why the program exists. The objective describes the outcome the program should achieve.

2 · Make success measurable

Separate execution progress from final success criteria. Schema validation is a milestone; 100% required telemetry migrated is a program outcome.

OBJECTIVE → SUCCESS CRITERIA → EVIDENCE

Leading indicators help predict success; lagging metrics confirm the outcome already achieved.

3 · Set explicit scope boundaries

In scope: 20 required telemetry schemas, validated 12M peak capacity, Security approval, operational readiness and legacy decommission readiness.

Out of scope: unrelated schemas, new telemetry capabilities, scaling beyond the agreed 12M requirement, and unrelated downstream redesign.

4 · Clarify ownership

The TPM owns program orchestration—not every technical deliverable.

TPM
Structures execution, aligns owners, tracks milestones, dependencies, risks and decisions.
Engineering Director
Executive sponsor / major decision owner.
Technical teams
Own platform, pipeline, security and operational deliverables.

5 · Prioritize constraints

Two constraints pose the greatest threat to the six-month objective: telemetry reliability cannot materially degrade, and the platform must support the 12M peak workload.

12M capacity↓Performance + reliability validation↓Security approval↓100% migration

6 · Use the charter as a baseline

If leadership later asks for 19M events/sec, do not silently absorb the request. Treat it as a scope change and evaluate business need, technical feasibility, schedule, resources, security, operations and risk.

CHANGE REQUEST → IMPACT ANALYSIS → OPTIONS → RECOMMENDATION → DECISION → UPDATED BASELINE

LESSON 4 CHEAT CODE

WEEK 1 · LESSON 5

Stakeholder alignment & influence without authority.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

Alignment does not require everyone to agree. A TPM uncovers stakeholder interests, clarifies decision ownership, quantifies tradeoffs, creates workable options and escalates unresolved conflicts to the right owner.

POSITIONS→INTERESTS→EVIDENCE→OPTIONS→RECOMMENDATION→DECISION

1 · Map influence and interest

Stakeholders care about different outcomes. Engineering may protect schedule, Security protects detection integrity, SRE protects operational reliability, Finance tracks cost, and leadership owns the broader business outcome.

HIGH INFLUENCE + HIGH INTEREST → MANAGE CLOSELY

2 · Separate positions from interests

A stated position is not always the underlying need. “Finish in two days” may mean protect schedule. “We need five days” may mean preserve three days of required validation while managing a resource conflict.

Solve for the interests before arguing over the positions.

3 · Influence with evidence

Challenge assumptions on both sides. Ask what is technically required, what is normal practice, what happens if a date moves, what can run in parallel, and which risks remain after mitigation.

CONSTRAINT ≠ PREFERENCE · REQUIREMENT ≠ HABIT

4 · Quantify the tradeoff

In the executive scenario, standard Security validation required five business days. With dedicated support and parallel Engineering preparation, full required coverage could be completed in three days.

At ~$80K per five-day week of dual-platform operation, three days cost ~$48K and save ~$32K versus the standard validation timeline.

5 · Respect decision boundaries

Program authority does not automatically override technical approval. An Engineering sponsor can own delivery decisions while Security owns security approval and SRE owns operational-readiness requirements.

Escalate ownership conflicts; do not silently override another function's gate.

6 · Escalate a decision, not a disagreement

Bring the right owners a clear decision: the conflict, evidence, business and technical impacts, viable options, recommendation, residual risk and the decision needed.

RIGHT DECISION → RIGHT OWNER → RIGHT INFORMATION → RIGHT TIME

7 · Stakeholder Alignment Map artifact

Cloud Security Data Platform Migration · Sanitized Learning Simulation

Engineering Director · High influence / high interest: Owns major delivery tradeoffs. Primary interest: complete migration and reduce dual-platform cost. Engagement: decision-ready options, quantified impact and explicit asks.

Security Analytics · High influence / high interest: Owns telemetry validation and security approval. Primary interest: detection completeness and reliability. Engagement: involve before cutover decisions; clarify minimum evidence and safe acceleration options.

SRE · High influence / high interest: Owns operational readiness and production support expectations. Primary interest: monitoring, incident response and rollback coverage. Engagement: validate staffing assumptions, residual risk and escalation paths before launch.

Engineering · High interest: Owns migration execution. Primary interest: readiness, schedule and efficient use of engineering capacity. Engagement: trace schedule impacts, parallelize preparation and clarify rollback responsibilities.

Finance · Consulted: Quantifies business impact of continued dual-platform operation. Engagement: convert vague cost pressure into decision-quality numbers.

TPM · Driver: Connects interests, evidence, dependencies and ownership; develops options and recommendations without taking over another team's technical authority.

8 · TPM tools in practice

Stakeholder Map · BuildRACI / DACI · PracticeDecision Log · PracticeProgram Charter · ConnectMiro / Lucidchart · Know

The artifact matters more than the software. A stakeholder map can live in Miro, Lucidchart, Slides or a spreadsheet; RACI/DACI and the decision log connect stakeholder influence to explicit ownership and decisions.

LESSON 5 CHEAT CODE

Understand the interest · Test the constraint · Quantify the tradeoff · Respect ownership · Escalate the decision

WEEK 2 · LESSON 1

Building an executable program plan.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

A useful program plan exposes meaningful milestones, the dependencies between them, the path controlling delivery, and where work can safely overlap without hiding risk.

MILESTONES→DEPENDENCIES→CRITICAL PATH→FAST-TRACK→FLOAT→FORECAST

1 · Milestones are meaningful states

Translate task lists into outcomes leadership can reason about: production infrastructure ready, application integration complete, Security approved, operational readiness approved, and launch complete.

Track the state the program must reach, not every activity required to get there.

2 · Model dependencies precisely

A dependency should describe what deliverable or milestone blocks another outcome—not simply which team comes first.

PLATFORM READY → FINAL APP INTEGRATION → SECURITY APPROVAL → SRE APPROVAL → LAUNCH

3 · Find the critical path

The critical path is the longest dependent path controlling the earliest possible completion date. It can move as forecasts change.

Technical W10 + Compliance W8 → Technical controls launch. If Compliance slips to W11, Compliance becomes the critical path.

4 · Fast-track safely

When Platform slipped, Application did two weeks of independent work in the test environment while networking remediation continued. Only final integration waited for the production-like environment.

Before adding people or cutting scope, inspect the dependency chain for safe parallel work.

5 · Understand float and contingency

Float is how much a task or path can slip without moving overall completion. Contingency is schedule capacity protecting the broader commitment.

Compliance at W9 with Technical at W10 has roughly one week of float. A move into W10 may consume float without delaying launch.

6 · Separate target, forecast and commitment

A better forecast does not automatically justify tightening the commitment. In the exercise, W10 remained the target/forecast while W12 stayed committed because Platform confidence was 80% and fast-tracking introduced rework risk.

TARGET ≠ FORECAST ≠ COMMITMENT ≠ DEADLINE

WEEK 2 · LESSON 2

Dependency & RAID management during execution.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

Execution control means detecting change early, decomposing it into RAID entries, assigning the right owner, tracing downstream impact, defining escalation triggers, and closing issues only after the resolution is validated.

DETECT→CLASSIFY→OWN→MITIGATE→ESCALATE→VALIDATE→CLOSE

1 · Decompose one event into RAID

A third-party API authentication change created multiple program-control entries rather than one generic problem.

MAY HAPPEN → RISK · TREATING AS TRUE → ASSUMPTION · CURRENT REALITY → ISSUE · RELIES ON → DEPENDENCY

Risk: integration testing may fail. Assumption: migration can complete in 3–5 days. Issue: legacy authentication will be retired. Dependency: a core application workflow requires the API.

2 · Track dependency health, not just existence

A useful dependency record includes the owner, needed-by date, current forecast, downstream milestones, status, and escalation trigger.

A dependency can exist for months; what matters during execution is whether it is healthy enough to protect the program plan.

3 · Technical owner ≠ TPM owner

Application Engineering owned the compatibility fix. The TPM owned schedule coordination, downstream impact assessment, dependency/status updates, communication, and escalation when intervention was needed.

TEAM OWNS THE FIX · TPM OWNS PROGRAM IMPACT & ORCHESTRATION

4 · Issue severity drives program status

A failed Security test became an issue because it had happened, but the program remained Yellow while root cause was unknown and recovery time remained.

An issue does not automatically make a program Red. Change status based on material impact to milestones, forecast, commitments, or required approvals.

5 · Accelerate without reducing quality

Security reduced validation from four days to two by assigning a second engineer to independent test suites. Test coverage remained unchanged, and the other review had sufficient float.

The value was not simply speed: earlier validation created recovery time before the legacy authentication expired.

6 · Close only after validation

Identifying a test-configuration root cause did not close the issue. The sequence was correction → rerun → pass confirmed → Security approval → issue closed → dependency satisfied.

IDENTIFIED ≠ RESOLVED · FIXED ≠ VALIDATED · VALIDATED → CLOSE

7 · Predefine escalation triggers

Escalation should occur when a decision or intervention is needed beyond the team's authority—not simply because a problem exists.

SRE confidence dropped from 90% to 60%. The TPM monitored actively, then escalated to the SRE Manager when the team confirmed an additional engineer was required to protect the W10 forecast.

8 · Make the escalation decision-ready

State the problem, impact, recommendation, decision owner, and needed-by time.

PROBLEM → IMPACT → RECOMMENDATION → DECISION OWNER → DECISION DEADLINE

9 · RAID & Dependency Management Log artifact

Cloud Service Launch · Sanitized Learning Simulation

Resolution principle: Close issues only after the fix is implemented and validated; do not treat root-cause identification as resolution.

WEEK 2 · LESSON 3

Execution control & operating rhythm.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

Keep program execution predictable with a lightweight cadence, immediate exception management, explicit handoffs, evidence-based status and decisions owned at the right level.

DETECT CHANGE→ASSESS IMPACT→ALIGN OWNERS→ACT / DECIDE→VERIFY→REPORT

1 · Run a useful operating cadence

Use a weekly 30-minute cross-team review with one lead each from Platform, Application, Security, Compliance and SRE. Teams retain their own standups; leadership receives a concise weekly asynchronous program update.

CHANGES → IMPACT → DECISIONS → OWNERS → DATES

Pre-read milestone and backlog status. Spend meeting time on changed forecasts, cross-team dependencies, blockers, risks, decisions and follow-ups—not task-by-task reporting.

2 · Manage exceptions between meetings

Urgent dependency changes should be communicated immediately, not saved for the next review. Confirm the deliverable, accountable owner, needed-by handoff time, acceptance criteria and downstream consequence.

An API promised for “Tuesday” was insufficient when Application required it by Tuesday at 9:00 a.m. to preserve end-to-end testing.

3 · Be firm without personalizing conflict

When a document handoff was missed and the response was dismissive, the TPM returned to observable facts, the Security review's five-day requirement, and a specific delivery commitment.

FACTS → IMPACT → REQUEST → OWNER → DEADLINE

Address recurring communication failures through clear expectations and a written record; do not confuse an unpleasant interaction with a reason to escalate.

4 · Separate workstream from program health

Compliance could be Yellow due to a two-day evidence delay while overall launch remained Green with three days of float. Reassess when new facts reduce recovery time or threaten required approvals.

Status is about program impact and confidence, not the count of issues or stakeholders' preferences.

5 · Use metrics with denominators

Overdue dependencies rose from 2 to 8 while tracked dependencies rose from 10 to 40: the overdue rate stayed at 20%. Later, 5 of 50 were overdue, improving the rate to 10%.

OVERDUE RATE = OVERDUE ÷ TRACKED × 100

Check tracking consistency, aging, on-time completion and milestone predictability before claiming execution improved or declined.

6 · Prioritize impact over raw counts

Platform had 4 overdue of 30 (13.3%); Application had 1 of 5 (20%). Platform still deserved investigation first because its overdue dependencies averaged eight days and three were on the critical path.

Normalized rates support fair comparisons; critical-path impact and aging determine intervention priority.

7 · Fast-track without skipping quality gates

Application prepared test cases while Platform repaired the API. Security performed design review Monday and completed remaining authentication validation in parallel with Tuesday's end-to-end testing, subject to full coverage and approval.

Parallelism reduces waiting, not required test scope. Confirm timing and handoff acceptance before calling the milestone achievable.

8 · Turn a workaround into evidence

Two available engineers implemented and tested overdue Platform work with a senior engineer's walkthrough, qualified review and a defect contingency. A successful one-off recovery is not yet a standard process.

Validate repeatability, engineering capacity, quality and outcomes through a pilot before recommending permanent adoption.

9 · Program Operating Rhythm & Execution Control Plan artifact

Cloud Service Launch · Sanitized Learning Simulation

Cadence: Weekly 30-minute cross-team dependency/decision review; team-level daily standups continue; weekly async leadership status; urgent exceptions reported immediately.

Attendees: TPM and designated Platform, Application, Security, Compliance and SRE leads; subject-matter experts attend only for relevant decisions.

Agenda: 0–5 min milestone changes; 5–10 dependencies and blockers; 10–15 new/changed risks; 15–25 decisions; 25–30 owner/action/date confirmation.

Source of truth: Program milestone view and dependency tracker linked to Jira/Azure DevOps work items; explicit owners, handoff deadlines, forecast confidence, acceptance criteria, downstream impact and escalation triggers.

Exception rule: Any cross-team deliverable required for a milestone is tracked even when healthy. Material changes trigger immediate owner-to-TPM communication and downstream impact assessment.

Decision discipline: Resolve within the owning team where possible; surface options, risk and decision deadlines when resource priority or technical approval exceeds that team's authority.

Quality safeguard: Schedule recovery may overlap independent work but cannot bypass required integration tests, Security validation or technical approval.

WEEK 2 · LESSON 4

Risk-based prioritization & tradeoffs.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

When resources are constrained, prioritize by launch impact, mandatory gates, dependency timing and evidence—not stakeholder pressure. Present options and consequences to the appropriate decision owner.

ASSESS IMPACT→MAP CONSTRAINTS→SEQUENCE WORK→RECOMMEND→DECIDE

1 · Critical versus desirable

Platform's production capacity issue and Security's mandatory approval were launch gates. The Application dashboard already met minimum requirements, so its improvement could be deferred without reducing launch readiness.

LAUNCH GATE ≠ EXECUTIVE PREFERENCE · QUALITY REQUIREMENTS ARE NOT OPTIONAL

2 · Engineer-days reveal capacity gaps

Platform requested two engineers for three days (six engineer-days); Security needed one engineer for two days (two engineer-days). Two engineers over three days provide six engineer-days, so doing both simultaneously was infeasible without changes.

Do not assume that assigning one person to each workstream preserves the original durations.

3 · Sequence work around deadlines

Two qualified engineers can work together on Platform Monday–Wednesday, completing before Friday's deadline. One then supports Security Thursday–Friday, ahead of its following-Wednesday approval deadline.

This uses timing flexibility to protect both launch gates without inventing additional capacity.

4 · Respond to competing priorities

Acknowledge the Application manager's executive-demo request, explain why launch gates take precedence, and offer a capacity review rather than an unapproved start-date commitment.

ACKNOWLEDGE → EXPLAIN IMPACT → OFFER FEASIBLE NEXT STEP

5 · Escalate a decision, not a complaint

When the Engineering Director requests the dashboard improvement during the same week, provide options: preserve Platform and Security staffing, or reallocate capacity and reassess launch readiness. Recommend the former while asking the authorized owner to decide on any changed priority.

An executive priority change does not silently waive mandatory Security or technical approvals.

6 · Clarify decision rights

The TPM analyzes impact, builds the allocation proposal and coordinates delivery. Resource owners approve assignments; authorized leadership accepts business tradeoffs; Security and technical owners retain required approval authority.

TPM RECOMMENDS · RESOURCE OWNER ALLOCATES · AUTHORIZED OWNER DECIDES

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

Capture proposed changes, assess the impact on the approved baseline, recommend options to the authorized decision owner, then record the decision and align delivery commitments. A preliminary estimate is not an approved customer promise.

REQUEST→ASSESS→OPTIONS→RECOMMEND→APPROVE→REBASELINE

1 · Establish the change request

Approved baseline: Five cloud security capabilities by Week 12, six engineers, Security validation in Weeks 10–11.

Requested change: Add a sixth capability for an enterprise customer. It is valuable but not contractually required at the Week 12 launch.

2 · Quantify the impact

The new capability requires two engineers for three weeks, including testing. Those engineers are assigned to two original capabilities. Engineering confirms all six cannot be completed by Week 12 with current staffing.

Two new engineers might start in Week 9, but need approximately one week to onboard; Security cannot guarantee validation by Week 12.

3 · Change Control & Decision Governance Register

WEEK 3 · LESSON 1

Technical architecture & system dependencies.

✓ COMPLETE
Core idea

End-to-end readiness is limited by the weakest required component. Distinguish tested capacity from real traffic, validate proposed workarounds, and require both technical and program readiness before migration.

INGESTION→QUEUE→PROCESSING→STORAGE→GO / NO-GO

1 · System Dependency Map — baseline

Security Telemetry Pipeline · Hypothetical TPM Learning Simulation
Ingestion · 15M/s→Queue · 14M/s→Processing · 9M/s→Storage · 13M/s

Requirement: Sustain at least 12M events/sec end to end. Bottleneck: Processing demonstrated only 9M/sec, 25% below target. At 12M/sec arrivals and 9M/sec processing, the queue accumulates 3M additional events/sec if excess events are buffered without loss or flow control.

Interpretation: Component figures are independently tested maximum sustained rates, not observed traffic or automatic event-loss counts. A 15M/sec ingestion capacity and 14M/sec queue capacity do not imply 1M events are dropped.

2 · Distributed-system vocabulary

Buffering
Temporarily holding events until downstream processing catches up.
Backpressure
Flow control that signals upstream systems to slow down when downstream capacity is constrained.
Throttling
Deliberately limiting the rate of requests or events.
Event loss
Data failing to reach its required destination; a growing backlog alone does not prove loss.
Horizontal scaling
Adding service instances; throughput gains must be demonstrated, not assumed to scale linearly.

3 · Evaluate the workaround

Proposal: Increase Processing from 10 to 20 instances, with an estimated capacity of 13M/sec and incremental cost of $40,000/month. Code optimization needs about three weeks; migration is targeted in two weeks.

Recommendation: Keep the migration date as a target, conditional on representative end-to-end load testing and operational validation. Do not treat estimated throughput as verified.

Test sustained throughput, queue growth, latency, integrity, failure recovery, monitoring, rollback and downstream behavior.

4 · Technical readiness is not program readiness

Load test: 12.5M events/sec sustained; average latency 2 seconds against a 3-second limit; zero event loss observed; stable queue backlog.

Open gates: Finance approval for incremental spending and SRE monitoring/alerting validation. Recommend holding migration until both are complete, while assessing whether the two-day delay affects launch.

5 · Final Go/No-Go recommendation

GO: Finance approves $40K/month for up to two months; SRE completes monitoring and alerting validation; Engineering verifies 12.5M/sec; Security reports no blockers.

Schedule: Migration slips two days, with five days of contingency still available. The committed launch date remains achievable, not guaranteed.

Follow-up: Monitor throughput, queue health, latency and reliability; track remaining schedule buffer; complete optimization and scale back before the approved cost window ends.

Decision record · Hypothetical learning simulation
